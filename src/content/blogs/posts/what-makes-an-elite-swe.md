## Increasing your breadth

- You have to focus of the fundamentals and get good at what you're doing in depth but don't dismiss everything else that's happening.
- The greatest skill you can have is the ability to learn fast and effectively in a short period of time. This means you have to learn how to dig deep into anything new you encounter.
- Have mastery in a few things but also take time to look into other areas of study.

## The ability to learn

- With how fast technology adapts, shifts and moves, you can't just survive by learning a technique, you have to adapt a method to learn new things.
- You have to learn how to work with new tech.
- Don't just be a fool with a tool. Or in other words, don't just know a tool, but insted grow the ability to use tools to get work done. Things change quick in tech, so if you're just the guy that know a certain tool and it get deprecated, you're now the laughing stock. So learn how to adapt to using new tools, don't just learn the tools.
- You also have to learn how to triangulate information and know what's real. We're now in the age where bots and AI produces more fake content and real humans do and so the margin of truth from lies is increasing. Have the ability to know what's true and what's not.

## Understand buzz words

- Most times, sales people and tech companies will use buzz words to just mean the same thing. Like AI is just deep learning on steroids, big data is just data but now in multiple and separated servers instead of one central server and so on.
- You have to understand not just the words being used, but what they actually mean and what problems they're talking about or solving.
- When dealing with clients, you have to understand what they mean more than just what they say. They could say they need you to build x but they instead mean y and are using x since that's what they understand as the meaning of y. Don't just take orders and build something, understand what the problem is and how to solve it efficiently.

## Good vs bad architects

- Bad architects are easy to spot. Good arthitects are the ones that just make things work magically well and nobody knows exaclty why.
- As an arthitect, don't just be the smartest person, be the person that makes everyone else smarter.
- You should be able to absorb the context, things that people explain to you, uncover blindspots, help people see different point of views, different angels and trade off they might be making without being aware of.
- Be an amplifier to the team.
- Don't just adopt new technology for the sake of it. First asses whether it's an improvement of what you have and why. Use what works, use what is proven and then use it in an effective way. So the wisdom of a good swe is when you're able to tell when to use a new technology.

## Stop building for 100x scale on day one

- Start small and then scale. First design for 100 or 1000 users.
- Only start to scale once you hit the limit of what your resources can do.

## Simple is complicated enough at scale

- When you're working with something at large scale it's already hard enough to think things through and even harder when those things are abstracted. So keeps things stupid simple and easy to reason about.
- This also involves the tools you use, you shouldn't have to think about memory leaks or garbage collection and all that when you still have to think about the business logic you want to implment. Like the way Go simplifies this for you. So choose the simple tools as well.

## Design for the next order of magnitude

- The technologies we use nowadays hardly last for the next 10 or more years, so build for the next order or magnitude then evolve it from there. But this is obviously not great for investors since they can't really forecast or plan ahead with confidence due the fuzziness, but from a software perspective, it's necessary.

## How to talk to non technical stakeholders

- You'll never convince stakeholder from a technical perspective since it's too abstract for them. Get closer to their side instead of trying to make then understand you.
- Understand the business constraints and discuss investment and solutions according to those constraints.
- Approach the discussions by first understanding the stakeholders.
- Take an example of building a software solution for business people that deal with truck and are using your software, they just want the button they clicked to work, they don't understand why they would click on it and not get any response or issues instead of what they expected. So understand the problems you're solving deeply from the perspective of those you're building the solution for.
- Understand the industry, then you can discuss your solution from the perspective of those in that industry from that vantage point.

## Use AI to validate, not to generate

- You should be able to understand and explain what the model produces or else you'll have a big problem expaning and maintaining what you're building.
